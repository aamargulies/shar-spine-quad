How did Skylake do in comparison with Graviton and EMR?

[Corrected 2026-10-11 against Text/corrections_2026-10.md; earlier
numbers were retracted, see that file. Every figure below is sourced
inline.]

At n=4096, tuned+spine (the shipped general-n spine path on each host),
per-cell medians of 5 ./bench 4000 5000 runs (bench_table.txt, from the
archived {host}_runs/rebench_run{1..5}.txt):

                      warm ns   cold ns   SKX ratio warm   SKX ratio cold
  SKX  (Xeon 8175M)      26.8     132.7       1.00x            1.00x
  EMR  (Xeon 8559C)      18.0      37.2       0.67x            0.28x
  GV4  (Neoverse V2)     16.2      34.6       0.60x            0.26x

(ratios computed from those medians.)

SKX is slowest in both columns: ~1.5x EMR and ~1.7x GV4 warm, ~3.6x EMR
and ~3.8x GV4 "cold" at n=4096. But the cold gap is not a core
comparison. bench.cpp's "cold" column had no cache flush when these were taken
(one was added on 2026-10-11): it walked the arrays in allocation order
with the same targets the previous algorithm had just used, and its ~35 MB footprint fits the last-level cache of every
host except the Pi 5 -- SKX's 33 MB L3 included (corrections section 5).
So all three servers' cold cells are last-level-cache hits in an
unflushed harness, and the 0.28x / 0.26x ratios compare cache levels,
not cores. Why SKX's LLC-hit cell is 3.6x EMR's is unexplained pending a
flushed cold column. Two candidate factors, not conclusions: (a) it is a
shared-tenant EC2 instance with noisier neighbours, and (b) it has 2
load AGUs and a narrower OoO window. (The 8175M's L3 is also close
enough to the footprint that I would want the flushed column before
trusting the LLC-hit label itself -- my own inference, not the
corrections file's.) What is *not* a factor is the AVX-512 frequency
license: the shipped SKX build is gated to AVX2 and executes no 512-bit
instruction at all, so nothing is downclocked (and a light 512-bit
integer op would trip license L1, not L2, anyway). EMR and GV4 are in
the same ballpark as each other (EMR slightly slower warm and cold) --
LLC-hit regimes in this column, as is SKX.

Relative wins from the optimizations, with sources:

  compile-time n=4096 hybrid vs general-n spine (warm / cold):
    SKX   -32% warm, 5/5; cold it is slower in 3 of 5 runs (per-run -12%
          to +62%, paired per-run median +40%) against a general-n
          control that is stable to +-0.6% in the same runs
          (skx_runs/hybrid_run{1..5}.txt; corrections 2.3), so the
          hybrid may carry a real cold cost; not settled at 5 runs.
    EMR   -63.6% / -34.5%  (emr_runs/shipped_summary.txt, 5-run medians:
          6.50 / 24.36 vs the same-run general-n spine 17.86 / 37.20)
    GV4   -57%   / -15%    (gv4_runs/hybrid_summary.txt: 16.22 -> 7.00
          warm, 35.86 -> 30.62 cold)

  two-level spine D vs A (outer spine + compile-time inner vs bsearch
  outer + general-n inner), paired per-run medians of the archived
  2026-05-18 runs, {host}_runs/twolevel_paired.txt:

                 hot_bat        hot_ser        cold_bat       cold_ser
    SKX          -59.9% (5/5)   -35.1% (5/5)   -24.1% (5/5)   -13.6% (4/5)
    EMR          -41.8% (5/5)   -18.3% (5/5)    -3.9% (5/5)    -3.7% (4/5)
    GV4          -30.9% (5/5)   -14.1% (5/5)   -18.1% (5/5)   +14.8% (0/5, A faster)

The earlier version of this note quoted D vs A as "-41% warm / -74%
cold" on SKX and "-40% / -71%" on EMR and called the structure
uarch-agnostic. Those numbers came from the retired two-mode harness
whose cold query missed the outer keys ~99% of the time and never ran
the inner search (corrections section 3); they are withdrawn. With the
4-mode harness and paired analysis the hot_bat gain is -60% on SKX
versus -42% on EMR (levels: SKX A 235.33 -> D 95.58 ns, EMR A 136.62 ->
D 79.30 ns hot_bat, per-cell medians, {host}_runs/twolevel_summary.txt),
and the cold gain is small: -24% on SKX, -4% on EMR, and on GV4 D is
actually slower than A in cold_ser (0/5). The structure is not
uarch-agnostic; it buys back the most on the narrowest core hot and
little or nothing cold.

Best variant per mode by paired rank (corrections 2.1): SKX F / F / C
(D 4/5 over F) / D (5/5 over F); EMR I (-0.7% vs F, a tie by size) / F /
C (5/5) / C (4/5); GV4 I (5/5) / F (5/5) / C (5/5) / C (5/5). So F is
the hot_ser choice on all three servers and the hot_bat choice on SKX;
on GV4 the Eytzinger variant I leads hot_bat (-10.8%, 5/5; measured
with the mis-aimed prefetch, pending re-measurement) and on EMR I ties
F by size. A bsearch or spine outer (C or D) is the cold choice; "F
wins every mode" is not supported on any of them.

The compile-time-n=4096 story also reverses the earlier reading. The
"~0 / -1.5% on SKX" figure described the retired unroll; the shipped
hybrid is -32% warm on SKX in 5/5 runs -- a real win, though about half
the size of EMR's (-63.6%) and GV4's (-57%). Where SKX differs is cold:
the hybrid is slower there in 3 of 5 runs (per-run -12% to +62%)
against a stable control. The prior prediction was "biggest win on SKX"
(narrow OoO -> branch control should hurt more); the data say SKX gets a
smaller warm win than the other two servers, and that its cold column is
where the straight-line hybrid can lose -- not settled at 5 runs.
