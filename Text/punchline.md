Roughly yes, with one caveat worth stating.

**The "quad search" work this project riffs on is entirely about the second (inner) layer.** Lemire's post presents `simd_quad` as a faster way to do membership on a *single* array container, the sorted `uint16_t[]` of at most 4096 elements. The post doesn't address the outer `keys[]` lookup (binary-search the high-halves to find which container you're in). That layer just calls `std::lower_bound` or CRoaring's equivalent and moves on. In that sense, "Lemire focuses on the inner and not the outer" is a fair characterization of **this specific piece of work**.

**The caveat:** Lemire is one of the principal authors/maintainers of CRoaring itself. He designed the overall two-level structure, the container-type dispatch, the bitset/array/run trichotomy, and the membership/intersection/union algorithms across all three. So it's not that he's *unaware* of the outer layer; he built the data structure. It's that, in the body of published algorithmic work on "how to search faster inside Roaring," the attention has historically gone to the inner operations: SIMD intersections between array containers, bitset-array hybrid intersections, and the `simd_quad`-style membership test. The outer `keys[]` has been treated as "short sorted array, use bsearch" and not optimized further.

**Why that's a defensible focus:** the inner array container is the hot path by volume. A boolean query over two Roaring bitmaps does one outer lookup to find matching containers, then many inner operations *per* container (a single `ANDNOT` on two 4096-element array containers is already thousands of u16-level comparisons). If you speed up the thing that runs 4000× per call, you get a bigger win than speeding up the thing that runs once. The outer is also small: 512 keys is a kilobyte, so textbook bsearch is already fast enough that it's easy to dismiss.

**Where this project extends his work:** the contribution isn't revisiting simd_quad; that's already his. It's noticing that the *outer* layer, which Lemire treats as settled, has a 55-year-old branchless improvement sitting on the table. Variant F is a two-layer composition: his SIMD trick on the inner, Shar's 1971 cmov chain on the outer. And the outer change is where the biggest remaining wins came from on five of the six hosts. So "Lemire focused on the inner" is the setup; "and the outer turned out to have more slack than anyone was checking for" is the punchline.
