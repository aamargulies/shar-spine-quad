Short version: the two layers are each well-established in isolation, but the
combination applied to Roaring-style nested containers isn't something I can
point to in the literature. A few specific angles:

- Single-level spine (packing every Nth key into a dense contiguous side-array
  so interpolation probes stream sequentially) is a classical implicit B+ tree
  / Eytzinger-style layout, known for decades. Khuong & Morin's "Array layouts
  for comparison-based searching" (2015) covers that family. The spine is
  essentially the top one or two levels of an Eytzinger tree flattened out.
  Novel-ish piece: using it alongside SIMD block-compare at the leaves
  (gap = one cache line of u16s, block-check in one SIMD compare) is a
  specific Roaring-fitting combination Lemire's original SIMD quaternary post
  (the `article.text` in this repo) didn't do.

> Correction 2026-10-11: "gap = one cache line of u16s" assumes cache-line
> alignment that no kernel or harness enforces: std::vector allocations are
> 16-byte aligned, so about 75% of 64-byte blocks and 87.5% of 128-byte blocks
> straddle two lines, and a 256-byte spine usually spans five lines
> (Text/corrections_2026-10.md, section 5).

- Applying the same pattern at the container-key level (the outer spine over
  `keys[]` in `bench_twolevel.cpp`) is where it gets more unusual. Roaring-
  style container sets classically use plain binary search over the outer key
  array. There are plenty of papers on index-structure layouts (CSS-trees,
  FAST, ART, learned indexes, etc.), but the specific maneuver of "take the
  inner-spine trick that works for u16 container contents and copy it
  wholesale one layer up to the u16 container keys, then keep the two spines
  in sync" isn't something I've seen written up. The fact that it wins big
  in both regimes independently (cold driven by the outer-spine streaming,
  warm driven by the inner unroll) and stacks multiplicatively is the
  interesting empirical observation.

> Correction 2026-10-11: "wins big in both regimes" rests on the retired
> two-mode harness, whose cold query missed the outer keys about 99% of the
> time and which had no cache flush between variants
> (Text/corrections_2026-10.md, section 3). Under the 4-mode harness with
> paired per-run analysis (5 runs per host, {host}_runs/twolevel_paired.txt,
> sections 2.1-2.3) the outer spine does not WIN hot anywhere by the >=3%
> paired criterion (archived runs); in the repaired Pi 5 re-runs D is the hot
> best-by-mean-rank (fastest in 7/10 hot_bat, 8/10 hot_ser), tied with F by
> size (F vs D +2.7% / +2.0%; pi5_runs/twolevel_paired_v2.txt, section 6) --
> Shar's outer (F) beats D hot 5/5 on five hosts (-14% to -54%; 4/5 for SKX hot_ser,
> -16.3%) and 4/5 at -6% on the Pi 5 in the archived runs (a tie in the
> repaired re-runs, as above) -- and
> the cold picture is mixed: D (outer spine) wins cold_bat 5/5 on the Pi 5 and
> cold_ser 5/5 on SKX, while the plain bsearch outer (C) beats F 5/5 in both
> cold modes on EMR and GV4 (C is fastest in 5/5 GV4 runs and 5/5 EMR cold_bat
> runs, 4/5 EMR cold_ser runs). So the two layers do not stack the way this
> paragraph describes. The compile-time inner is now the hybrid, not an unroll.

- Not-novel caveats: (a) multi-level sparse indexes are ancient (textbook
  database stuff); (b) the idea of a small "fence" or "landmark" array per
  block is in many B-tree variants; (c) cache-aware two-level search is the
  whole motivation for CSS-trees (Rao & Ross 2000). So the underlying
  structure is not new. What's specific here is the combination with
  SIMD-quad block check at the leaves, the cache-line-sized block that lines
  up gap=32 for 64 B lines and gap=64 for 128 B lines, and the empirical
  result that it tracks cleanly across both SKX (AVX2 block check,
  downclocked scalar loop) and EMR (zmm single-compare, full-frequency
  scalar loop).

> Correction 2026-10-11: on SKX the shipped gated build executes no zmm
> instruction at all, so its scalar loop is not downclocked (512-bit integer
> compares would trip license level L1, not L2) (Text/corrections_2026-10.md,
> section 5). The alignment caveat above also applies to "the cache-line-sized
> block that lines up gap=32 for 64 B lines and gap=64 for 128 B lines".

If you're thinking about a write-up: I'd pitch it as "an old idea (multi-level
index) applied at a new granularity (32-element u16 blocks) in a new home
(Roaring container sets) with a specific SIMD leaf" rather than a
fundamentally new data structure. The measurement story is strong --
3.4-3.85x cold-cache improvement that replicates across two very different
x86 uarchs -- which is what would carry the post.
